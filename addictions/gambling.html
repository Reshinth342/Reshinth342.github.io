Gambling Disorder Explained

Gambling Disorder is a recognized impulse-control condition characterized by repeated betting despite severe financial distress, debt, and relationship breakdown. The psychological illusion of 'chasing losses' drives individuals deeper into financial hardship.

⚠️ Myth vs Fact: Gambling Losses Cannot Be Won Back

Gambling apps and casinos rely on mathematical house edges. Betting more money to recover previous losses is a cognitive trap that escalates debt. Gambling losses cannot be recovered by simply gambling more.

Common Warning Signs

  • Chasing losses (gambling larger sums to win back previous lost money).
  • Hiding debts, loans, or casino activities from family or partners.
  • Borrowing money, taking out high-interest loans, or selling assets to gamble.
  • Obsessive checking of sports betting odds or casino promotions.

🌿 Self-Exclusion & Financial Recovery

Utilize national self-exclusion registries (e.g. GAMSTOP / state gaming blocks), hand financial control temporarily to a trusted partner, and contact Gamblers Anonymous or non-profit debt counselors.
